# Designing for tvOS

> People enjoy tvOS’s vibrant content, immersive experiences, and streamlined interactions in media, games, fitness, education, and home utility apps.

## Device characteristics and patterns

- **Display:** A TV typically has a very large, high-resolution display.
- **Ergonomics:** People generally remain many feet from a stationary TV—often 8 feet or more—but may interact while moving around the room.
- **Inputs:** People can use remotes, game controllers, voice, and apps on other devices to interact with Apple TV. See the [Siri Remote](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/remotes), [game controllers](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls), and [Siri](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/siri) guidance.
- **App interactions:** People may stay immersed for hours, while also using picture-in-picture to follow another app or video.
- **System features:** Integrate with the [TV app](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-video#Integrating-with-the-TV-app), [SharePlay](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/shareplay), [Top Shelf](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/top-shelf), and [TV provider accounts](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/managing-accounts#TV-provider-accounts).

## Best practices

- Support fluid, familiar gestures through the Siri Remote.
- Embrace the tvOS focus system: items gently highlight and expand as people move among them, helping them know what to do and where they are.
- Deliver edge-to-edge artwork, subtle fluid animation, and engaging audio for a rich, cinematic experience that remains clear, legible, and captivating across the room.
- Make sign-in easy and infrequent, handle shared sign-in, and switch profiles automatically when the current viewer changes.
